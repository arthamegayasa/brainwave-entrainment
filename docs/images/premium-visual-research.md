# Premium visual direction for SwaraSanti

Observed first-party product pages on 26 September 2026. The observations below describe *their* pages; the direction after them is an original proposal, not an asset brief. No imagery, video, illustration, or layout is to be copied from these products.

## What the pages actually show

| Source | Visual evidence | Transferable lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Endel homepage](https://endel.io/) | Near-black hero, spare centered copy and one CTA above a grouped device/product composition; the mobile section explicitly describes each soundscape as having its own generative visual. Its own artwork often uses fine abstract lines. | High contrast, generous emptiness, and one clear focal point can make a digital audio product feel intentional. **Do not carry over the fine-line pattern** that the current SwaraSanti design needs to leave behind. |
| [Portal homepage](https://portal.app/) and [Experience](https://portal.app/experience) | Edge-to-edge waterfall and forest environments, small overlaid navigation and large editorial type. A named location anchors the hero; the feature section calls its nature imagery “Motion Visuals” and describes window-like views. | A place, atmospheric depth, and a legible foreground hierarchy create immersion more readily than a waveform. Treat motion as ambience, not a constant attention demand. |
| [Calm homepage](https://www.calm.com/) | Mountain-lake photography leads the page; below it, a bright open surface holds a short headline, prominent action, and simple goal-led sections (“Stress less,” “Sleep more,” “Live mindfully”). | An expansive scene can establish mood while plain, readable content and clear goals do the interaction work. Rest between visually rich moments matters. |

## One original direction: **The Quiet Horizon**

Build SwaraSanti as a small set of *imagined atmospheric places*: original paintings (created with GPT Image for this app, not photographs, borrowed brand assets, or downloaded video) with procedural SVG/CSS layers pinned over them. The visual language is a broad color field, a low horizon or spatial boundary, one soft light source, and two or three depth planes. It expresses a destination and time of day, **not a plotted frequency**. This can accompany real-time synthesized audio without implying that audio files or real-world recordings exist.

- **Composition:** Give the landing hero one dominant environment with large negative space for a short goal-first invitation and primary action. Cards crop genuinely different scenes, not one template recolored eight times; preserve a quiet, high-contrast area for labels and controls. The player keeps playback state and controls unmistakably foregrounded.
- **Environmental imagery:** Use distinctive silhouettes and spatial cues—still lake and distant moon, dawn through trees, low dunes and rising light, or clouded sky—made from filled forms, haze and broad gradients. Keep detail sparse at small card sizes. Avoid repeated hairline waves, audio-spectrum motifs, and tiny technical diagrams as hero art.
- **Color and restraint:** Start from deep ink/blue-gray neutrals; use a controlled cool-to-warm progression across goals, with one restrained luminous accent per scene. Keep typography and interactive elements consistently legible rather than letting colorful artwork compete with them. Reserve bright surfaces for deliberate content relief, as Calm does after its scenic opening.
- **Purposeful motion:** If motion is present, drift a haze plane or modulate a light bloom slowly; hold the horizon steady and make the resting frame complete. Pause offscreen, avoid rapid pulses or audio-reactive jitter, and honor reduced-motion preferences with a finished static composition. Motion should suggest time and atmosphere, not visualize purported brain activity.

This is a design translation of the cited composition and mood strategies, **not** a claim that SwaraSanti has Portal's location footage, Calm's photography/content, or Endel's adaptive visual technology. The implemented paintings and their provenance are documented in the [image notes](README.md#in-app-scene-paintings).
